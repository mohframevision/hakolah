## Why Do So Many People Quit Free Apps After One Day?

The usual advice is: "Can't afford Adobe? Use the free stuff." So someone opens GIMP expecting a Photoshop clone, finds it completely different, closes it and never comes back. The reason is that most free apps don't replace a whole paid app — they replace one piece of it. Pick the wrong app for the piece you need, and you're back to paying.

## Photos: GIMP to Edit, Krita to Paint

Photoshop does two jobs: photo editing and digital painting. In the free world, each has its own app. **GIMP** for editing, cutouts and layers; **Krita** for painting with a pen and tablet, built by painters for painters. Together they cover most of what Photoshop does. To edit without installing anything, **Photopea** runs in the browser and opens PSD files.

## Lots of Camera Photos: darktable, Then GIMP

Editing one photo in GIMP is fine, but a shoot of four hundred RAW photos needs a developer and library like Lightroom. That's **darktable**: sort, rate and develop your photos without touching the original file. Then send only the shots that need detailed pixel work to GIMP.

## Logos and Print: Inkscape, Then Scribus

**Inkscape** is the Illustrator alternative for logos and icons: its vector shapes scale from a tiny icon to a billboard without blurring. But it has no native CMYK print workflow, which is why print shops sometimes bounce the file. The fix is to move the design into **Scribus**, the InDesign alternative, which supports CMYK and press-ready PDFs. If you want one app combining photos, vectors and layout, **Affinity** is now completely free.

## Video Editing: Start Easy, Then Level Up

For your first video, use **OpenShot**: drag and drop, and finish the same afternoon. When it slows down on long projects, move to **Shotcut** or **Kdenlive**. And once you start talking about cinematic color grading, the free **DaVinci Resolve** is the same software used on Hollywood films.

## 3D: Blender

**Blender** isn't "good for a free app" — it's simply one of the best 3D apps there is. The Oscar-winning 2025 film Flow was made entirely in it.

## Bottom Line

Don't look for a free copy of Adobe — look for the right app for each piece: GIMP and Krita for photos and painting, darktable for camera photos, Inkscape and Scribus for logos and print, OpenShot then DaVinci for video.
